Not a promise. A missing permission.

Most apps ask you to trust that they won't misuse what they see. Winkle QR doesn't hold the INTERNET permission, so Android itself refuses to let it open a network connection. A QR code can contain your Wi-Fi password, your address book entry, a payment link. This app reads it on your phone and nowhere else.

How the privacy claim is kept honest

Anyone can say "we don't track you". Here's what stands behind it.

  • No internet permission. Android grants network access through a permission this app doesn't request, so the operating system blocks it at the lowest level. You can check on your own phone: Settings → Apps → Winkle QR → Permissions.
  • The build refuses to ship otherwise. Every build inspects the finished app and fails if any permission beyond the camera appears — including ones a third-party library tries to add quietly.
  • The scanner is inside the app. The barcode model ships with the app, so nothing is downloaded and no scan is sent away for recognition.
  • Your history is excluded from backups. It isn't copied to Google Drive or moved to a new phone.
  • Handing off is your choice. Opening a link, joining Wi-Fi or saving a contact passes the details to an app you already trust, only when you tap the button.
  • No ads, analytics, crash reporting or account. There is nothing to collect, and nowhere for it to go.
